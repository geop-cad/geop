Scope: SMLib `src/SmFillet*.cpp`, `SmBlendSurface.cpp`, `SmCurveFilletExecutive.cpp`,
`SmBrep::CreateCornerBlend/CreateEdgeEdgeBlend/DeleteFilletFace`, vs geop
`ops/geop-ops-fillet` (`blend.rs`, `rolling.rs`, `corner.rs`, `tool.rs`, `operation.rs`),
`lib/geop-lib-sweep` (skin), `ops/geop-ops-sketch/src/editor/drawing.rs` (2-D fillet),
`ops/geop-ops-surface` (boundary/Coons).

Paths below: SMLib = `/home/tobiasjacob/usd-brep/source/SMLib/`, geop = `/home/tobiasjacob/geop/`.

## How the two differ in architecture (read first)

- **SMLib** builds an explicit *fillet sheet* (`m_pFilletBrep`): one `SmFilletSolver` per edge
  (marching the offset-surface intersection), one `SmFilletCorner` per vertex, classified by
  `SmFilletCorner::Create` (`SmFilletCorner.cpp:1129`) into ~12 corner classes, each of which
  builds corner topology, vertex geometry, and a patch; then the sheet is stitched and merged
  into the target (piecewise merge, topology insertion or global merge; `SmFilletExecutive.cpp:2676, 2917`).
  Special cases (rollover, big radius, degenerate corners, squeezed edges) are explicit
  topology rewrites of that sheet.
- **geop** builds a *tool solid* per edge (or one joined tool per corner/mitre component) and
  cuts it away / fills it in with a boolean (`blend.rs:1294 blend`, `blend.rs:1835 apply_tool`).
  The boolean takes care of "what the fillet consumes"; everything the tool construction cannot
  express is refused by name. So the port is mostly about **making more tools** (or tool
  pieces) rather than porting SMLib's sheet surgery.

## 2. Special cases to port (prioritised by how often a user hits them)

**Status after round 2.** One fillet path: every edge is rolled. The
extruded and revolved tools are gone; the rolled skin interpolates each
section multiplied by the edge's weight, so a section carried unchanged
along a line or round a circle is reproduced exactly, and such a blend is
left untilted (tangent, as the swept one was). P4 (mixed convexity: the
odd edge first, then the two as one chain round its round), P12 (refused
by name where a face bends more tightly than the ball), P14 (partial arcs
are exact by the same weights where the edge is an exact arc), the P15
169° upright and lofted-blade trailing edge are done. A rolled ball now
rolls on its faces carried on past a cliff, which P3's cuts then trim.
Still open: P7, P9, the N-sided corner patch, setbacks, two-radius/conic
sections.

Each: SMLib location, the case and SMLib's handling, geop today, geop-style sketch.

### P1. Straight edge between planes ending at an oblique or curved wall / third face
- **SMLib**: `SmFilletNx1Corner::MakeCornerTopology` (`SmFilletCorner.cpp:5990`) + `CreateCornerPatch` (`:5908`): any third face; the fillet end is the fillet's intersection with the (extended) side face (`SM_FE_FILLET_X_SIDE_FACE`, `SmFilletEdge::CalcFilletIntSideFace`, `SmFilletGeom.cpp:6168`).
- **geop**: `blend.rs tool_end` (`:363-478`) returns `End::Wall { unless: Some(..) }` / `End::Flush { unless: Some(..) }` for a non-square plane or curved face; for a *straight edge between planes* that is then refused (`blend.rs:329`, `:1421`) with the message "a straight edge between planes ends flush only with a plane square to it ... an edge rolled between curved faces with any wall". The rolled path already handles it (`rolling.rs run_ons :2428`, `Rolled::trims`).
- **Do**: In `plan_edge` (`blend.rs:1092`), plan a straight edge as `Planned::Rolled(chain)` whenever either of its `tool_end`s comes back with `unless: Some(_)` and no mitre will take it. That is the same decision the module doc already states; it only needs to be made before choosing Swept. Cases: box edge running into a cylindrical boss, rib edge into a sloped wall, top edge of a plate meeting a drafted wall. No numerics change: the rolled tool for a straight edge between planes is exact at its stations and its arcs (rational quadratics), cubic in between, within `DEVIATION`. Optionally later: keep the exact extruded section and only roll the last span (reuse `rolling::run_on`).
- Regression: block with a cylinder boss, fillet the block's top edge that runs into the boss.

### P2. Decide contact containment over the whole contact curve, not at its midpoint
- **SMLib**: `SmFilletSolver::TestRollOver` (`SmFilletSolver.cpp:5714`) classifies each rail against its face (`SmCurveClassification`) and drives `ProcessRollOver` from the intervals.
- **geop**: `blend.rs check_touches` (`:745`) checks **one point halfway along the edge** (`check_touch` -> `face_contains`). The ignored test `fillet_of_a_post_joint_whose_contact_runs_off_the_bar` (`regression_tests.rs:150`) shows the consequence: the contact circle (r=4.5) runs off a bar 8 wide, the midpoint is inside, and an **invalid solid** is built. This also violates AGENTS.md "Do not decide from samples".
- **Do**: For swept tools the contact curves are exact (lines along straight edges, circles of the revolve). Intersect each contact curve with the face's boundary coedges in `(u, v)` (pcurve x pcurve subdivision, `curve_curve_intersect` on the contact's pcurve vs the face's loop) and require: no crossing in the open curve (`Inside` everywhere), touching only at its ends where the end condition says so. For rolled tools, intersect the padded contact curve (`enclosing_pad`) with the boundary. A crossing becomes a refusal naming the crossed edge (and its convexity: needed for P3), never a silent build. This both fixes the invalid-model bug and produces exactly the data rollover needs.

### P3. Rollover over a crease: cliff (opposite convexity) and crash (same convexity)
- **SMLib**: `SmFilletSolver::ProcessRollOver` (`SmFilletSolver.cpp:7161`). For each rail interval leaving the face (classification "unknown" at its midpoint), find the side edge hit (`sm_FindRolloverSideEdge` `:6103`) and its convexity relative to the filleted edge (`same_convexity_edges` in `SmFilletCorner.cpp:443`):
  - *tangent* side edge: switch the offset surface to the neighbour face (geop has this).
  - *same convexity* (fillet crashes into the neighbour face, e.g. fillet along a box edge whose contact on the top runs into a raised wall): the fillet surface is trimmed by the adjacent face (rail becomes fillet x side face).
  - *opposite convexity* (cliff: the contact runs off the face over a convex edge, e.g. post-under-bar): the side face is extended (`CreateCliffRollOverSideFG` `:6518`, `bTrimSideFaces` decided by `IsConvexRadialSector`) to cut the fillet, a support edge is added at a corner (`sm_ComputeSupportEdge` `:6342`), the rail is split (`sm_SplitRail` `:5999`) and the cliff rail trimmed (`SmFilletGeom::TrimCliffRails` `SmFilletGeom.cpp:10764`). A small side patch (`SM_FG_CLIFF_SIDE_PATCH`) fills the hole. The "roll the ball along the edge" alternative is noted but not implemented.
  - adjacent corners whose vertices move are recomputed (`rAdjustedCorners`).
- **geop**: swept refuses "too large for face" (or builds invalid, P2); rolled refuses "meet at a crease" (`rolling.rs:1398`) or "touches no pair of the faces" (`:538`).
- **Do (tool + boolean makes this simpler than SMLib)**:
  - *Cliff, convex filleted edge*: the tool is material to remove. Where the contact runs off face A over a convex edge into the air, the tool may simply **run on**: carry face A's surface on past its boundary (as `carried_on_past` `rolling.rs:707` already does at chain ends) and let the boolean cut; the part of the tool hanging past the cliff cuts air. What must not happen is that the tool cuts into the cliff's other face B. Cut the tool by B's surface carried on (the same `Rolled::trims` machinery: split the tool along a sheet of B, keep the piece on the solid side). This is exactly SMLib's "extend side face and trim the fillet".
  - *Crash, same convexity*: the ball would have to touch three faces; the blend's contact on A ends where the fillet meets B. Trim the tool by B (same split), and the boolean imprints the fillet x B curve. If B is also tangent-continuous with A, this is the existing tangent rollover.
  - Decide "cliff vs crash" by the bend of the crossed edge (`blend::bend` at the crossing point found in P2), three-valued: if `bend` could be either, refuse naming the edge.
  - Regression tests: drawer-handle post under a narrower bar (exists, ignored); fillet along the base of a boss whose contact runs into a neighbouring rib (crash).

### P4. Full corners of mixed convexity (fillet every edge of a pocketed/bossed block)
- **SMLib**: `SmFilletCorner::Create` routes an NxN corner with any concave edge to `SmFilletConcaveNxNCorner` (`SmFilletCorner.cpp:1440`; topology `:5196`). The two rails on the face between the two same-convexity edges do not meet within the edges' ranges; they are extended and joined by a rail-rail interpolation edge (`SM_FE_RAIL_RAIL_INTERPOLATION`, `SmFilletEdge::CalcRailRailInterpolation` `SmFilletGeom.cpp:6665/7165`, conic or cubic Hermite between them, `SmFilletGeom.cpp:1086-1175`), validated by `TestRailRailInterpolationCurve` (`:5339`), then a Coons/N-sided patch closes the corner (`CreateCornerPatch` `:1955`).
- For 3 edges / 2 filleted of opposite convexity (L-block): `SmFillet3x2MixedConvexityCorner` (`:4623, 4755`) joins the "on edge" and "on extended edge" curves and builds a 3-sided patch.
- **geop**: refused, `corner.rs:161` "some are convex and some concave" (test `pocketed_block_fillet_every_edge_is_refused`, `tests.rs:337`). "Fillet all edges" on any part with a pocket/boss/step hits this.
- **Do**: The geometric fact: at a corner of a pocket rim (rim edges convex, upright edge concave), the concave fill's tool and the two convex cuts' tools overlap near the vertex; the blended surface between them is a patch tangent to all three blends. In tool terms: build each edge's tool run on past the vertex (convex cuts: `End::RunOut`/mitre; concave fill: flush with the wall), then add a **corner tool** bounded by the three blends' end sections and a G1 N-sided patch (port `SmBSplineSurface::CreateCornerBlend` / a Gregory or Coons-with-cross-derivative patch into `geop-core-geometry`, reusable by `geop-ops-surface/boundary.rs` too). Simplest honest first step: the **bevel** fallback (P8): run the tools long and let the boolean intersect them, which is what SMLib does when `m_bBevel`. Name the corner patch `fillet(F,V,corner)` like the ball.

### P5. Full corners with different radii, and variable radius into a corner
- **SMLib**: NxN corners don't need equal radii: the rails of adjacent fillets are intersected (`SmFilletVertex::CalcRailIntRail` `SmFilletGeom.cpp:1465`) and a Coons/N-sided patch is built (`CreateCornerPatch` `SmFilletCorner.cpp:1955`); the analytic sphere is only tried when it fits (`MakeAnalyticCornerSurface` `:3379`, refused for variable radius and blend-curve sections). Variable-radius edges entering a corner are temporarily treated as constant (`SmTemporaryRadiusChange` `SmFilletExecutive.cpp:4968`).
- **geop**: `corner.rs:184` refuses unless all fillets meet with one radius ("give the corner's vertex a radius of its own").
- **Do**: For three different radii at a convex 3-face corner the classic answer is a 3-sided patch bounded by the three fillets' end sections, where each fillet ends at the cross section through the pairwise rail-rail intersection (vertex where two contact curves on a shared face cross). Interval-style: find the contact-curve crossings on each of the 3 faces by subdivision + Newton (`curve_curve_intersect` of the two contact pcurves on that face; isolate then refine, per AGENTS); the end section of each fillet is its section through those two points; the patch is a G1 3-sided blend (same new primitive as P4). The joined-tool machinery in `tool.rs build_tools/corner_faces` (`:230, :423`) generalises: replace the spherical triangle by the patch, keep the apex patches.

### P6. Big radius on a short edge (two corners merge)
- **SMLib**: `SmFilletExecutive::TestAndConvertBigRadFillet` (`SmFilletExecutive.cpp:4264`): detects a fillet between two Nx2 corners whose rails run in opposite directions (end sections cross), squeezes one rail and merges both corners into one corner patch; corner edges that vanish are removed by `SmFilletCorner::SqueezeEdge` (`SmFilletCorner.cpp:2264`, `SM_FE_TO_BE_SQUEEZED`).
- **geop**: `corner.rs:302` refuses "the ball rounding the corner ... touches its faces {setback} along it, past its other end".
- **Do**: Detect it exactly as geop already does (setback past the edge's other end, an interval comparison), and instead of refusing, treat the two vertices and the short edge as **one corner** of the faces around both (4 faces for a box's short edge): this is then a 4-sided corner (P7). Until P7 exists the refusal is right; keep it.

### P7. Corners where 4 or more edges meet; 4-edge vertices with 2 or 3 filleted
- **SMLib**: `SmFilletConvexNxNCorner` for any N (patch from `SmBrep::CreateCornerBlend` which returns one patch per side for N >= 5, `SmBrep.cpp:2488`), `SmFilletNx2Corner` for N = 4 (the two filleted edges must not share a face: one fillet x fillet curve, `SmFilletCorner.cpp:6487`), `SmFillet4x3Corner` (`:8570`, blending patch). (SMLib itself has `Nx1` with N > 3 "not yet implemented".)
- **geop**: `tool_end` (`blend.rs:412`) "a blend ends only at a corner of exactly three faces" (two coplanar flush faces count as one wall); corners `corner.rs:167, 251`, `tool.rs:431`.
- **Done (round 3)**: `tool_end` lets a convex edge run out past a vertex of any number of faces when it leaves the solid through every one of them (measured against the face it leaves least squarely), and `plan_corners` bevels a corner of four or more filleted edges, as for different radii. Tests: `fillet_of_two_opposite_edges_into_a_pyramids_apex`, `fillet_of_every_edge_into_a_pyramids_apex` (two roofs crossed). `NurbSurface::locate` keeps a pole row's parameter for a point that could be the pole. Left: the same pyramid lofted to a point (`fillet_of_every_edge_into_a_lofted_pyramids_apex`, ignored): its faces' apex is a pole of their parametrization, and carried on past it for the run-out the pole row lies inside the domain, where `normal` does not find it. Concave corners of four faces, and four faces with an edge running into a wall, are still refused; the smooth N-sided patch is not there (the bevel stands in).
- **Planned**: For 4 convex edges meeting at an apex with equal radii a ball touching four planes generally does not exist (overdetermined): an N-sided G1 patch is required (new primitive from P4). For N=4, 2 filleted on opposite faces: both tools run out (as for 3 faces) and the boolean makes their crease; this needs only `tool_end` to accept 4 faces when the edge leaves the solid through *all* the other faces at the vertex (every other face's `squareness` definitely > 0): a run-out cuts air there regardless of how many faces. That is a cheap partial port.

### Setbacks and the smooth corner patch (round 3: left)
- No N-sided G1 fill exists in `core/` or `lib/` yet (`geop-ops-surface`'s boundary surface is an operation, which the fillet may not call, and tangent only for four sides). A setback corner is that patch with `2N` sides: each fillet ending a setback from the vertex along its edge (`SmFilletVertex::CalcSetBackVert`: the plane square to the edge at that arc length), and the two contacts on each shared face joined between the ends (`SM_FE_RAIL_RAIL_INTERPOLATION`). Without the patch a setback has nothing to close the corner with, so neither is built; corners of different radii stay bevelled. Reproducer: `rolling_tests::cube_corner_with_mixed_radii_is_rounded_smoothly` (ignored), asserting the `...,corner)` patch.

### P8. Bevel corner option, and bevel as the fallback where no patch exists
- **SMLib**: `m_bBevel` / `SmFilletExecutive::SetBevelCorner` (`SmFilletExecutive.cpp:4127`): fillet surfaces run long and intersected pairwise, meeting at a centre vertex (`SM_FV_FILLET_X2_FILLETS`, `SmFilletVertex::CalcFilletInt2Fillets` `SmFilletGeom.cpp:2669`); `CalcCornerGeom` skips the patch (`SmFilletCorner.cpp:1693-1700`). Nx2 sets bevel automatically when the fillets aren't tangent (`:6568, 6779-6814`).
- **geop**: not offered for full corners (always the ball, or refused).
- **Do**: A per-vertex `corner: Ball | Bevel` choice in `FilletArgs` (one enum field keyed by vertex, like `vertex_radii`). Bevel = every edge's tool runs out past the vertex (the "outward corner" path already used when not all edges are filleted) and the boolean intersects them. This immediately unlocks P4/P5 configurations as bevels while the G1 patch is not there.

### P9. Edge ending where its faces become tangent (vanishing fillet)
- **SMLib**: `SmFilletNx1Corner::CheckNx1Degeneracy` (`SmFilletCorner.cpp:5850`) classifies the corner as `SmFilletDegenerateCorner` when the two side faceuse normals are parallel at the vertex; `CheckAndFixDegeneracy` (`:1736`) merges all fillet vertices into one (`SM_IP_SINGULARITY` tsect points), so rails converge to the vertex.
- **geop**: `rolling.rs chain_bend` (`:2206`) and `blend.rs:296` refuse "faces could meet tangentially ... no corner to blend". Typical case: a rib or boss that merges tangentially into a wall, a cylinder sitting tangent on a plane up to a tangent point.
- **Done (round 3)**: a chain ends where its faces could be tangent at a vertex (`tangent::next_link`, `vanishes_at`), and `tool_end` says `End::Vanish` there whatever else meets. The last station is the vertex itself (the ball touching both faces there), and every station's apex is drawn in to lie as far beyond the ball's corner as the chord is long (`Station::pinched`), so the tool ends in a point (a skin apex). The boolean takes it unchanged. Stations stay evenly spaced: Newton near the vertex is ill-conditioned only as `1/angle`, and the doubling converged. Chamfers refuse it by name (a fixed-distance chord would lie in the faces). Tests: `fillet_of_an_equal_tee_vanishes_where_the_pipes_touch`, `fillet_of_an_equal_tees_whole_joint`, `fillet_of_a_bicylinder_edge_vanishes_where_the_pipes_touch` (convex, four edges at each vertex).
- **Planned**: The fillet width goes to zero as the dihedral angle goes to 180°; the ball's centre runs to the edge. Do not end the tool at the last station whose section is "small enough": that is an epsilon. The end of the tool is the point where the section degenerates; build the last span with a collapsed end row (a pole) at the vertex, as geop's revolve does at an axis (AGENTS "A pole is one vertex at many (u, v)"). The station positions approach the vertex geometrically (like the cone apex case in AGENTS "A step sized by the tightest bend never arrives"): fix the number of stations by a parametrization in the dihedral angle, not by step-halving. Medium effort; worth it because it appears in nearly every organic model.

### P10. Mitre at inward corners beyond the mirror-symmetric case
- **SMLib**: `SmFilletNx2Corner::AdjustConcaveCornerTopology` (`SmFilletCorner.cpp:7071`) and `Make4x2ConcaveCornerTopology` (`:6905`): rails extended (`SM_FE_RAIL_EXTENSION`, `SM_FV_RAIL_X_EXTENDED_EDGEUSE`, `CalcRailIntExtendedEdgeuse` `SmFilletGeom.cpp:2856`) and intersected or blended; works for different radii and free-form edges.
- **geop**: `blend.rs mitres/mirrored` (`:1173, 1223`): straight edges only, constant radius, sections mirror each other (`:1241, :1792`), no rolled blends (`:1404`), at most two edges (`:1186`).
- **Do**: For two different radii at an inward corner, the two tools' surfaces intersect along a curve (not a plane). Instead of a planar mitre, run each tool on past the corner and cut each by the other's *blend surface* (tool-by-tool split along a sheet of the other's blend face, the `trimmed` machinery in `blend.rs:1516`), then join. For rolled blends the same applies with the rolled tools. Keep the planar mitre as the exact special case when sections mirror.

### P11. Concave fill ending at an outward corner on an oblique/curved face
- **SMLib**: `SmFilletNx1Corner` (`SmFilletCorner.cpp:5908-6487`): "If the filleted edge is a concave edge, an 'end' surface will be created to fill the hole of the side face" (extended side surface, `SM_FE_ON_EXTEND_EDGE`, `CreateEndPatch` `:1882`).
- **geop**: refused, `blend.rs:457` "a fill up to it would end on the corner's edge between the third face and the face it is filled along".
- **Do**: The fill ends in the extension of the third face (its surface carried on, `carried_on_past`), capped in it: the cap is a piece of the third face's surface, which the boolean union merges with it. The trap named in the code (an end lying on the corner's other edge) is avoided by capping in the *surface* and letting the boolean imprint, not by ending the tool on the edge. Needs the "cap in a surface" variant of `tool.rs Cap` (currently flat only).

### P12. Explicit refusal when the radius exceeds the concave curvature (self-intersecting fillet)
- **SMLib**: `SmSelfIntersectionHandler` family (`SmFilletSolver.cpp:8157-8900`): smoothing or blending over the self-intersection; note `IsSelfIntersecting` is a stub that always says FALSE.
- **geop**: no check; fails somewhere in the rolling Newton or section building with an unrelated message.
- **Do**: Before rolling, compare the radius with `NurbSurface::curvature_radius_along` (`core/geop-core-geometry/src/nurb_surface/curvature.rs:76`) of each face, in the section plane, on the concave side, over each span (an interval bound, not samples). If `radius` definitely exceeds the curvature radius somewhere, refuse naming the face and where; if it could, refuse too (honest). SMLib's smoothing is not worth porting.

### P13. Interior C1-only knots of the edge/faces as forced stations
- **SMLib**: `LoadFilletSolver` collects internal G1 knots of the edge curve into `m_vG1Knots`; `CalcFilletGeom` adds through-points there and may split (`SmFilletSolver.cpp:1595-1640`).
- **geop**: stations break at vertices and face crossings only (`rolling.rs` module doc, `crossing`).
- **Do**: Add the edge curve's knots of multiplicity >= degree (and the faces' knot lines crossed by the contact curves) as forced station positions, breaking the spline there, exactly as `fit_pcurve` does per smooth piece (AGENTS "A hash map's order is no order", last paragraph). Reduces station counts and `DEVIATION` failures on fitted geometry (STEP imports, lofts).

### P14. Exact torus/cone fillets for partial arcs and plane-cone straight edges
- **SMLib**: `CalcTorusFilletGeom` (`SmFilletSolver.cpp:860`) for any circular arc between plane/cone surfaces; `CalcConeFilletGeom` (`:662`) for a line between plane/cone.
- **geop**: whole circles only (`circle_edges`, `blend.rs:582`); otherwise rolled.
- **Do**: Revolve the exact section through the arc's sweep angle instead of a full turn when the arc's ends are corners `tool_end` accepts (`End::RunOut` analog at angular ends: run the revolve on a little past each end angle, or flush with a plane through the axis). Exact geometry, fewer booleans of approximated surfaces. Low frequency but cheap.

### P15. Known geop bugs from ignored tests (fix independently of ports)
- `tutorial_regression_tests.rs:53` shelled box: outer vertical edges' contact edges split at the floor's inside height; the boolean reads the shell's offset inner floor as reaching the walls.
- `regression_tests.rs:87` 169° dihedral: `predictor_corrector_step` leaves the leaning face (fillet; chamfer builds).
- `regression_tests.rs:150` post under bar: invalid solid instead of refusal (P2/P3).
- `regression_tests.rs:468` lofted blade trailing edge: boolean misses an intersection curve (classify_face straddles).
- `subd_mouse_sole_rim_filleted` (round 3): the sole's patch corners where the crease runs on smoothly have `S_u` collinear with `S_v`. The normal there is now the corner's (`NurbSurface::corner_normal`), and the tangent chain takes a face's normal at its vertex where its loop puts it (`Model::normal_at_vertex`; locating the vertex ran into a valley of `(u, v)` some 1500 boxes long). Needs curvature without the metric's inverse, or no patch corner where the boundary runs on smoothly.

## 3. Missing whole features

1. **Fillet removal / defillet** (`SmFilletRemoval`, `SmBrep::DeleteFilletFace`, `SmFilletRemoval.cpp:124-1535`): identifies the 4-sided fillet face, its two tangent faces and two end faces, the side edges at the four corners (`CollectFilletTopology` `:208`), extends the tangent surfaces (`CreateExtendedSurface`), intersects them (`IntersectTangentSurfaces` `:812`), computes new corner vertices by surface/surface/surface intersection with the end faces (`CalcNewCornerVertices` `:465`), extends side edges (`FindSideEdgeExtensions` `:581`) and rewrites topology (`DoRemoval` `:1015`). geop: nothing. In geop style this is a `ModelOperation` in a new op (or `geop-ops-edit`): recognise a blend face (any face G1 to two neighbours along its long sides), extend the two neighbours (`geop-ops-surface/extend` exists), and rebuild by a boolean of the extended faces' half-spaces is not possible for free-form; the direct topology rewrite (replace face by the intersection edge) is the way. Also useful for the STEP-import repair workflow. Refuse when the end faces aren't three-face corners (SMLib has the same limit: "More than three edges: we do things differently").
2. **Face blend / surface-surface fillet** (`SurfaceSurfaceFillet`, `SmFilletExecutive.cpp:1306`): fillet between two faces (possibly of different bodies or sheets) not sharing an edge, with signed radius per side, cross-section linear/approx-circular/circular, trimming at ends `SM_BT_NONE/MINIMAL/MAXIMAL/BEVEL/BLEND`, base-surface trimming normal/reverse, mirror/complement. geop's `rolling.rs` already solves "ball touching two surfaces" by Newton at stations; what is missing is seeding without an edge (a spine from the offset-offset intersection: `geop-lib-booleans` face x face tracing on offsets, or `meet` from a user point) and the end trimming. A sheet result first, then an option to trim and knit the faces (`geop-ops-surface/knit`).
3. **Setback (vertex blend) corners** (`m_dSetBackDist`; `CalcSetBackVert`, `CalcSetBackRail`, `SM_FE_SETBACK_RAIL`): fillets end a setback distance from the vertex along each edge and a 2N-sided patch blends them; essential for good-looking corners where radii differ.
4. **G1 N-sided corner patch primitive** (`SmBrep::CreateCornerBlend`, `SmBSplineSurface::CreateCornerBlend`): needed by P4, P5, P7, setbacks, and `geop-ops-surface/boundary.rs` (which today is G1 only to planes).
5. **Edge-edge / 4-boundary blend surfaces with G1..G3 continuity** (`SmBrep::CreateEdgeEdgeBlend`, `SmBlendSurface`, `SmCrossBoundaryType`, `CreateDoubleBlendSurface`): a "blend surface" operation in `geop-ops-surface`.
6. **Cross-section shapes**: curvature-continuous / conic fillets (`SmBlendCurveCrossSectionFSG`, G2/G3, thumbweight; conic Rho is the industry name), asymmetric two-radius fillets (`SmConstantRadiusFS` two offsets), constant-chord-width fillets and chamfers (`SmConstantDistanceFS`), rolling-ball chamfer (linear section on const-radius rails). In geop these are all different `tool_profile` (`blend.rs:648`) and `section` (`rolling.rs:771`) shapes plus a different offset pair in `place` (`rolling.rs:437`): the station solver already takes two offsets per face in principle (`Offset`), so the asymmetric fillet is the cheapest. A conic section is a rational quadratic with weight `rho/(1-rho)` instead of the circle's cosine: exact and cheap for swept edges.
7. **Smooth variable-radius laws** (`SmBSplineFilletLaw`, laws through points with derivatives, laws across edge sequences): geop's `Radii` is piecewise linear by arc length with kinks at vertices; a cubic law (points + optional slopes) along the chain keeps the blend G2-ish and removes the forced station breaks at every vertex.
8. **Fillets on sheet bodies / open corners** (`SmFilletOpenCorner`): requires `tool_end` to accept laminar edges (the tool runs out past a free edge) and sheet booleans.
9. **2-D fillets of arbitrary curves** (`SmCurveFilletExecutive`): geop's sketch fillet works only at a corner of two lines (`drawing.rs:667 cut_corner`, `corner_lines`). Line-arc, arc-arc, spline corners and fillets between curves that don't meet (offset-offset intersection; SMLib tries all four offset sign combinations) are missing. In geop this belongs in the sketch solver: an arc with Tangent constraints to both curves and a Radius, with the trimming points as coincident constraints; the seed from the offset intersection.
10. **Chamfer corner patch** (`m_bChamfer`, planar corner face) and a **chamfer by distance and angle** (neither kernel has the latter; it is a one-line `tool_profile` variant in geop).

## Where geop is already better

- **Honest enclosures**: contact curves are padded to enclose the true contact (`NurbCurve::enclosing_pad`), stations are doubled until the skinned blend is within `DEVIATION` of the ball and checked to leave each face on the ball's side (`TILT`, "An approximate tangency dips through the face" in AGENTS). SMLib accepts tolerance-fitted rails (`dApproxTol`, 50*tol interval squeezing, `SM_EFF_ZERO_SQRT` parallel tests).
- **Exact analytic sections**: straight-between-planes and whole-circle blends are exact rational quadratics, extruded or revolved; the corner ball is an exact sphere piece; rolled stations are exact great arcs.
- **Consumption of neighbouring geometry for free**: the tool + boolean approach removes or fills whatever the blend overlaps (holes near the edge, small faces) without SMLib's piecewise/global merge modes (`m_bDoGlobalMerge` "catches large radius cases where original Brep pieces get cut away").
- **Station exactly on face transitions and at radius kinks** (`rolling.rs crossing`), closed chains broken at four stations, halves for chains ending where they start (`Rolled::halves`).
- **Refusals name entities and the condition** (`corners_of`, `corners_at`), where SMLib prints "Corner Case Not Implemented" and returns no corner, and its self-intersection detector is a stub.
- **Stress sweeps** (`stress_tests.rs`) require every built result to be valid and distinguish refusals from failures; SMLib has no equivalent in this tree.
